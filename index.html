How this model works & what to notice

A connected mechanical model

Ventricular pressure combines a timed contraction with an exponential passive pressure–volume relationship. An actively contracting atrium fills the ventricle. A two element Windkessel represents arterial compliance and peripheral resistance.

Valve flow = conductance × pressure difference. Forward conductance controls obstruction; reverse conductance controls leakage. Valve opening follows the pressure gradient.

Each change is simulated to a repeating beat. Playback shows that settled beat, not the transient response immediately after a change.

Reading your experiment

The dashed gray traces are the normal baseline. Wiggers overlays align by fraction of a cycle when heart rate changes. Stroke volume is EDV − ESV; cardiac output uses net aortic flow, so regurgitation can separate the two measures.

The ECG is schematic. This draft has no right heart, reflex regulation, detailed valve motion, heart sounds, or wave reflections. The displayed aortic trace includes a small schematic dicrotic notch timed to valve closure. This drawing adjustment does not alter simulated pressures, valve flow, P–V loops, or calculated values. Its depth decreases with valve leakage for illustration; it is not a quantitative prediction. Not every atrial pressure wave is reproduced. Filling pressure is maintained by an external reservoir; this is not a closed circulation.

Teaching prototype with illustrative parameters, not a patient prediction model. Method references: Closed-loop real-time simulation model of hemodynamics and oxygen transport in the cardiovascular system; Modeling the Instantaneous Pressure–Volume Relation of the Left Ventricle: A Comparison of Six Models. This is a simplified implementation, not a reproduction of either paper.
