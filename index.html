    How this model works & what to notice

    Heart, valves, and arteries

    The left atrium supplies blood to the left ventricle. Ventricular pressure combines timed muscle activation with passive stiffness. Ventricular contractility sets the strength of active contraction, while the relaxation time constant (τ) controls how quickly late activation decays. A larger τ means slower relaxation.

    Set atrial contractility to 0% to remove active atrial contraction and compare mitral flow, ventricular filling, and cardiac output at the same heart rate. The simulation keeps a regular rhythm and the atrium still fills the ventricle passively.

    Valve flow follows the pressure difference across the valve. Lower forward conductance represents greater obstruction. Reverse conductance allows leakage.

    The three element Windkessel combines proximal aortic impedance, arterial compliance, and peripheral resistance. Compliance lets the arteries store blood as pressure rises. Peripheral resistance controls how readily blood runs off into the systemic circulation.

    Reading the plots

    The P–V loop and Wiggers diagram show the same settled beat. Use the cursor to connect time, valve events, pressures, and volume. The dashed normal overlay in the Wiggers diagram uses the same elapsed time in milliseconds as the current beat and shows only the part of the normal beat that fits.

    Show Cardiac Phases marks the active beat on both plots. With valve leakage, ventricular volume can change during contraction and relaxation even when both valves are closed to forward flow.

    Positive mitral flow enters the left ventricle and positive aortic flow enters the aorta. Negative flow indicates regurgitation. The mitral E wave comes from early filling as the ventricle relaxes. The A wave comes from atrial contraction, which is at the start of the plotted cycle. Over a settled beat, net mitral inflow equals net aortic outflow.

    Stroke volume is end diastolic volume (EDV) minus end systolic volume (ESV). Ejection fraction is stroke volume divided by EDV. Cardiac output uses net aortic flow, so with regurgitation a large stroke volume can coexist with reduced forward delivery to the circulation.

    What the model represents

    The left heart receives blood from a reservoir at the chosen filling pressure. Systemic runoff drains to a fixed pressure boundary. The presets illustrate changes in left heart function and arterial load. Exercise combines changes in rate, contractility, relaxation, filling support, and resistance. The ECG and aortic notch are schematic.
